Odontogram in Odoo 19: how it works in Dental Clinic

The odontogram is the dental map of a patient: which teeth are healthy, which have caries or restorations, and what is planned for each. In Dental Clinic for Odoo 19 it is an interactive OWL component, used inside the consultation and on the patient record. This guide describes what it does today.

Where you find it

The same component is available in two places in the Odoo backend: inside the consultation form, next to the SOAP notes, and on the patient record. That means a dentist can update a tooth while documenting the visit instead of switching screens, and the consultation is linked to the change in the history. Reception, who only need the schedule, never have to open it.

FDI numbering

Teeth use the two-digit FDI notation: the first digit is the quadrant and the second is the position from the central incisor backwards.

  • Permanent teeth (32): quadrants 1 to 4, teeth 11 to 18, 21 to 28, 31 to 38 and 41 to 48.
  • Deciduous teeth (20): quadrants 5 to 8, teeth 51 to 55, 61 to 65, 71 to 75 and 81 to 85. They are available when pediatric dentistry is enabled in the clinic settings.

Thirteen tooth statuses

Every tooth has one status, drawn in its own colour and explained in a collapsible legend: healthy, decayed, filled, missing, root canal, crown, implant, in treatment, not erupted, exfoliated, worn, fractured and prosthetic.

Conditions per surface

Beyond the status of the whole tooth, you can record a condition on each of its five surfaces: mesial, distal, buccal, lingual or palatal, and occlusal or incisal. There are seven conditions: healthy, caries, restoration, fracture, attrition, sealant and erosion. Each tooth can hold one condition per surface. This is useful for pinpointing where a lesion or a restoration is.

Editing a tooth

Click a tooth to open its popover and change the status. For several teeth at once, switch on selection mode. You can pick teeth one by one or use shortcuts: select all, all molars, premolars, incisors or canines, the upper or lower arch, or a single quadrant. Then apply one action to the whole selection: change the status, report a problem or add a procedure.

Tooth records and notes

Each tooth also has its own form with a free-text notes field, the list of its surface conditions and a button that opens its full history. This is where a clinician keeps context that does not fit a status, such as a note about a restoration to watch at the next check-up.

Badges for open work

Small badges next to a tooth summarise pending work: open problems, planned procedures and completed procedures. The legend explains each one, so the chart doubles as a quick view of what remains to be done.

Change history

Every status change is recorded in the tooth history: previous status, new status, date and time, the user who made the change and, when relevant, the consultation in which it happened. History entries are read-only, so the evolution of each tooth stays intact over time.

Mixed dentition and pediatric transition

With pediatric dentistry enabled, a patient can have adult, deciduous or mixed dentition, and the module supports the transition:

  • Starting mixed dentition creates the permanent teeth as not erupted (first molars start as healthy) and links each deciduous tooth to its permanent successor.
  • Marking a deciduous tooth as exfoliated also activates its successor if it was not erupted.
  • Completing adult dentition marks the remaining deciduous teeth as exfoliated and moves not-erupted permanent teeth to healthy.

Patients see it too

In the patient portal, the "My Teeth" page shows the same odontogram in read-only mode. When JavaScript is not available, a static table with the same information is shown instead.

What it is not

The odontogram records information. It does not interpret findings or recommend treatment, and it does not replace the dentist's judgement. To see how it behaves, open the live demo, enter a consultation and change a few teeth: the history fills in immediately.
